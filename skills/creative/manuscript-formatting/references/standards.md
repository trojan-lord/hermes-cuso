# Manuscript Formatting & Editing Standards — Knowledge Bank

Condensed reference compiled from Kindlepreneur, Reedsy Blog, Chicago Manual of Style, Wikipedia typography articles, and the Hermes manuscript-formatting skill. Use this as the authoritative lookup when formatting queries exceed "extract from AI chat."

## 1. INDUSTRY-STANDARD MANUSCRIPT FORMAT

### Page & Layout
- **Page size**: 8.5" × 11" (US Letter), white background
- **Margins**: 1" all sides
- **Font**: 12pt Times New Roman, black, no highlighting
- **Spacing**: Double-spaced, NO extra space between paragraphs
- **Alignment**: Left-aligned (ragged right) — NOT justified
- **Indent**: 0.5" first-line indent on every paragraph
- **After periods**: Single space only (never double)

### Title Page
- Contact info: upper left (name, address, phone, email)
- Title: centered ~⅓–½ down page
- "By" + author name: centered below title
- Word count: rounded to nearest thousand
- Same font as body (12pt TNR), no graphics

### Header
- Format: `LASTNAME / TITLE — PAGE#` (right-justified)
- Page 1 = first page of body text (after title page)

### Chapters
- New page via page break (never repeated Enter)
- Heading centered ~⅓ down page: "Chapter N" on line 1, title on line 2
- Optional bold (check agent guidelines)
- 14–16pt bold or keep 12pt — be consistent
- First paragraph after heading: indented (standard) or un-indented (both acceptable if consistent)

### Scene Breaks
- Three centered asterisks: `* * *` on blank line
- Alternatives: `###` or `— — —` (pick one, use throughout)
- No decorative elements or extra blank lines

### File
- Format: .docx
- Naming: `Lastname_Title_Date.docx`
- All chapters in one document
- "END" or "#" centered after final line

## 2. EDITING LEVELS

### Developmental Editing (First pass, post-first-draft)
- Plot structure, pacing, story arc
- Character development, motivation, arcs
- POV choices, narrative structure, timeline
- Scene purpose, redundancy, chapter organization
- Theme consistency, worldbuilding logic
- Output: editorial letter (5–20+ pages) + chapter notes + margin comments
- NOT focused on: grammar, spelling, sentence-level prose

### Line Editing (After developmental)
- Word choice precision, cliché removal
- Sentence structure variety, rhythm, flow
- Tone/mood alignment, voice strengthening
- Show vs tell conversion, redundancy cutting
- Dialogue naturalism, anachronism checks
- Output: tracked changes + margin rationale

### Copy Editing (After line editing)
- Grammar, punctuation, spelling (including homophones)
- Consistency: names, places, dates, physical descriptions, timeline
- Style sheet creation
- CMOS (Chicago Manual of Style) as reference
- Fact-checking basics, formatting consistency
- Tense/POV consistency, continuity
- Industry catch rate: 90–95% (even the best miss things)

### Proofreading (After typesetting — final step)
- Typos, formatting errors, typesetting artifacts
- Consistency final check, missing text
- Page numbers, headers/footers, fonts
- Big Five publishers still find hundreds of errors at this stage

## 3. FICTION GRAMMAR & STYLE RULES

### Dialogue Punctuation (American English / CMOS)

**Periods and commas go INSIDE quotes. Colons and semicolons go OUTSIDE.**

1. **Complete dialogue + tag**: `"I'm tired," she said.`
2. **Tag before speech**: `She said, "I'm tired."`
3. **Action beat (not a tag)**: `"I'm tired." She collapsed.` (period + capital)
   - WRONG: `"I'm tired," she collapsed.` ("collapsed" isn't a tag)
4. **Split dialogue with tag**: `"I'm tired," she said, "and I want to sleep."`
5. **Exclamation/question with tag**: `"Go away!" she shouted.` / `"Where?" he asked.`
6. **Interrupted dialogue (em dash)**: `"I don't think we should—" She cut him off.`
7. **Multi-paragraph dialogue**: Opening quote at start of EACH paragraph; closing quote only at end of final paragraph.
8. **One-word dialogue**: `"No."` — punctuated as complete sentence.

### Em Dash vs En Dash vs Hyphen

| Mark | Use | Spacing (CMOS) | Spacing (AP/NYT) |
|------|-----|----------------|-------------------|
| `-` Hyphen | Compound words: well-known | No spaces | No spaces |
| `–` En dash | Ranges: pages 10–25; "to"/"through" | No spaces | Spaces |
| `—` Em dash | Interruptions, breaks, aside | **No spaces** | **Spaces** |

- CMOS (book standard): `word—word` (closed)
- AP/NYT: `word — word` (open)
- British English: spaced en dashes for parenthetical use
- Fiction: follow CMOS — closed em dashes

### Ellipses
- Standard: three periods, no spaces: `...` (U+2026 preferred)
- CMOS: no spaces, period at end if sentence ends: `I wonder....`
- AP: spaces between dots: `. . .`
- Never more than two ellipses in close proximity

### Italics — Do/Don't

**Italicize**: Titles of long works (*The Great Gatsby*), foreign words not yet English (*saudade*), internal thoughts in close third, words used as words, emphasis (sparingly: 1–2/page max), ship names (*Belfast*), scientific names.

**Don't italicize**: Titles of short works ("The Lottery"), quoted speech in narration, sound effects, fully adopted foreign words (café), overused emphasis.

### Tense Consistency
- Choose past (most common) or present — stick with it
- Flashbacks in past-tense fiction: use past perfect ("had walked") initially, then shift to simple past
- Watch for accidental shifts: "She walked to the store. She is worried." → "was worried"

### POV Consistency
- **First**: "I walked" — only knows what narrator knows
- **Third limited**: "She walked" — one POV per scene/chapter
- **Third omniscient**: narrator knows everything (requires skill)
- **Head-hopping**: Switching POV within scene without section break — most common POV error
- In close third, don't describe what POV character can't observe

### Show vs Tell

**Telling markers**: Emotion labels ("She was angry"), summary ("They had a great evening"), adverbs in dialogue tags ("she said angrily"), exposition dumps in dialogue, pure interiority without external anchor.

**Showing techniques**: Physical sensations ("Her jaw tightened"), action ("She slammed the door"), dialogue with subtext ("I'm fine." She turned away, blinking hard.), sensory detail, what characters DON'T say.

**Acceptable telling**: Transition scenes, time compression, minor characters, establishing facts quickly.

## 4. SUBMISSION GUIDELINES

### Agent Package
1. **Query letter** (250–400 words): hook, brief synopsis, comp titles, bio, personalized per agent
2. **Sample pages** (5–50 pages): first pages in standard manuscript format
3. **Synopsis** (1–5 pp, single-spaced): present tense, third person, includes ending
4. **Full manuscript**: complete, polished, in .docx — only sent on request

### Formatting Checklist for Submission
1. ☐ Title page: contact info, title, author, word count
2. ☐ 12pt TNR, double-spaced
3. ☐ 1" margins
4. ☐ 0.5" paragraph indents
5. ☐ Header: title/last name + page number
6. ☐ Scene breaks: `* * *` centered
7. ☐ Chapters: centered on new pages
8. ☐ Curly quotes, proper em dashes
9. ☐ Single space after periods
10. ☐ File: `Lastname_Title_Date.docx`
11. ☐ "END" marker
12. ☐ Word count on title page

### Genre Word Counts
| Genre | Range |
|-------|-------|
| Literary fiction | 80K–100K |
| Commercial/women's | 75K–100K |
| Science fiction | 80K–120K |
| Fantasy | 80K–120K (epic can exceed) |
| Thriller/mystery | 70K–90K |
| Romance | 50K–90K |
| YA | 55K–80K |
| Middle grade | 25K–55K |
| Picture books | 500–1K |

### Common Rejection Triggers
- Not following submission guidelines
- Unpolished manuscript
- Weak query hook
- Adverb overuse in dialogue tags
- Head-hopping / POV inconsistency
- "Said bookisms" overuse
- Infodumps
- Opening with weather/dreams/waking up
- Incomplete manuscript (for fiction)

## 5. TYPESETTING STANDARDS (Final Book Production)

### Kerning
- Adjusting space between specific character pairs
- Good kerning: visually uniform space between all pairs
- Problem pairs: AV, AW, AT, Yo, Va, We
- Professional tools handle kerning tables; fine-tuned manually

### Leading (Line Spacing)
- Vertical space between baselines of adjacent type lines
- Book body text: 120–145% of font size (e.g., 10pt on 12pt)
- Sweet spot: 1.3–1.5× font size
- Too tight = cramped; too loose = disconnected

### Widows and Orphans
- **Widow**: paragraph-ending line at top of new page (alone)
- **Orphan**: paragraph-starting line at bottom of page (rest on next page)
- **Runt**: very short final line (one word) anywhere
- Always fix in print; cannot control in ebooks (text reflows)

### Drop Caps
- Oversized initial letter at chapter start, spanning 2–4 lines
- Optional but common in trade books
- Design rule: if using drop caps, keep rest of chapter page simple

### Running Heads
- Even (verso): book title or chapter title
- Odd (recto): chapter title or author name
- Chapter opening pages: typically no heads or page numbers
- Page numbers: outer margin, smaller font (8–10pt)
- Front matter: Roman numerals (i, ii, iii...)

### Trim Sizes
| Size | Best For |
|------|----------|
| 4.25" × 6.87" | Mass market paperback |
| 5" × 8" or 5.25" × 8" | Trade paperback |
| 5.5" × 8.5" | Trade paperback (alternative) |
| 6" × 9" or 6.25" × 9.5" | Hardcover, large trade |
| 8" × 10" or 8.5" × 11" | Coffee table, picture books |

### Body Text Fonts
Garamond, Baskerville, Caslon, Times New Roman, Georgia, Minion Pro, Adobe Garamond Pro. Size: 10–12pt fiction, 10pt nonfiction, 14pt+ for large print.

### Additional
- Published books: fully justified body text (vs. manuscript left-aligned)
- Gutter margin (spine side) slightly wider than fore-edge
- Bleeds: 0.125" for any image/color extending to page edge
- Front matter: Roman numerals; body text: Arabic
- Paragraph indent: 0.3"–0.5" (skip after chapter heads and scene breaks)

## MANUSCRIPT vs PUBLISHED BOOK — Quick Reference

| Element | Manuscript | Published |
|---------|-----------|-----------|
| Font | 12pt TNR | Garamond/Caslon/etc. (10–12pt) |
| Spacing | Double | Single (1.3–1.5× leading) |
| Alignment | Left-aligned | Justified |
| Margins | 1" all sides | Varied (gutter wider) |
| Scene breaks | * * * | Ornaments or space |
| Chapters | 14–16pt bold centered | Drop caps, styled fonts |
| Page numbers | Header | Footer, outer margin |
| Headers | Title — Page# | Title (even) / Chapter (odd) |
| Page size | 8.5" × 11" | 5"×8" to 6"×9" |
| Purpose | Readable & editable | Pleasing to read |
